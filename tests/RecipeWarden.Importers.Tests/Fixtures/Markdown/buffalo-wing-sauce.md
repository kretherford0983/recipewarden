# Buffalo Wild Wings–Style Medium Sauce (Homemade)

A balanced, emulsified buffalo-style sauce with classic heat, tang, and body—ideal for wings, sandwiches, or dipping.

[Oil Based Sauce](#oil-based-sauce)

[Butter Based Sauce](#butter-based-sauce)

## Choosing the Right Sauce

To get the best results based on your cooking situation:

- **Use the Oil-Based Sauce** when:
  - You're prepping ahead and want to store the sauce in 12 oz squeeze bottles
  - You need it to last 2–3 weeks in the fridge
  - You want something easy to grab, reheat (or not), and go

- **Use the Butter-Based Sauce** when:
  - You're making wings now and want that bold, buttery, classic flavor
  - You're using the whole batch immediately (same day)
  - You don't plan to store leftovers (or only a small amount)

> 🧠 **Rule of Thumb:**  
> Oil-based = make-ahead, store, and reuse  
> Butter-based = immediate use, no leftovers

## Oil Based Sauce

This version is best when you want a **smooth, stable, restaurant-style sauce** that behaves more like a bottled wing sauce than a classic hot wing toss.

Use this recipe when:
- You want a **pourable or dippable sauce** that stays emulsified even when cold
- The sauce needs to be **made ahead of time** and stored for days or weeks
- You want consistent coating across wings, sandwiches, wraps, or fries
- You plan to **reheat and reuse** the sauce multiple times
- You want a texture closer to commercial Buffalo Wild Wings sauces

Characteristics:
- Thickens as it cools and remains smooth when refrigerated
- Emulsion is stabilized by egg yolk and cornstarch
- Less prone to separation during storage
- Slightly less “buttery” mouthfeel, more neutral and polished

This is the preferred option for **meal prep, parties, or batch cooking**, where reliability and shelf life matter more than traditional butter-forward flavor.

### Yield

- Approximately **1 1/2 cups** of sauce  
- Enough for **3–4 lb of wings**, depending on coating preference

---

### Prep Time

- **Prep:** 5 minutes  
- **Cook:** 5 minutes  
- **Total:** ~10 minutes

---

### Ingredients

- 1 cup Frank’s cayenne pepper sauce (e.g., Frank’s RedHot Original)
- 1/3 cup vegetable oil (or another neutral oil)
- 1 tsp granulated sugar
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1/2 tsp ground black pepper
- 1/2 tsp ground cayenne pepper (adjust to preferred spiciness)
- 1/2 tsp Worcestershire sauce
- 1 large egg yolk
- 2 1/2 - 3 tsp **cold** water
- 2 tsp cornstarch

---

### Instructions

1. **Combine ingredients**  
   In a small saucepan, combine the cayenne pepper sauce, vegetable oil, sugar, garlic powder, black pepper, cayenne pepper, and Worcestershire sauce.

2. **Heat the mixture**  
   Heat over medium heat, stirring constantly, until the mixture just begins to simmer. Remove from heat immediately.

3. **Create slurry**  
   In a separate medium bowl, whisk together the egg yolk, water, and cornstarch until smooth.  
   The egg yolk acts as an emulsifier for the oil and water-based ingredients.

4. **Temper and combine**  
   Slowly pour the warm sauce mixture into the egg yolk slurry while whisking continuously to temper the egg and prevent scrambling. Mix until fully combined.

5. **Cool and thicken**  
   Allow the sauce to cool completely. The sauce will thicken as it cools.

6. **Store and serve**  
   Refrigerate in an airtight container until ready to use. Reheat gently before tossing with cooked wings or using as a dipping sauce.

---

### Ingredients (16oz Extra-Thick Batch)

- 1 1/3 cups Frank’s cayenne pepper sauce (e.g., Frank’s RedHot Original)
- 7 tbsp (or 1/3 cup + 1 tbsp) vegetable oil (or another neutral oil)
- 1 1/3 tsp granulated sugar
- 1 1/3 tsp garlic powder
- 2/3 tsp ground black pepper
- 2/3 tsp ground cayenne pepper (adjust to preferred spiciness)
- 2/3 tsp Worcestershire sauce
- 1 large egg yolk + 1 tsp egg white
- 4 - 5 tsp **cold** water
- 4 tsp cornstarch

---

### Instructions

1. **Combine ingredients** In a small saucepan, combine the cayenne pepper sauce, vegetable oil, sugar, garlic powder, black pepper, cayenne pepper, and Worcestershire sauce.

2. **Heat the mixture** Heat over medium heat, stirring constantly, until the mixture just begins to simmer. Remove from heat immediately.

3. **Create slurry** In a separate medium bowl, whisk together the egg yolk (plus the teaspoon of egg white), cold water, and cornstarch until completely smooth.  
   The egg yolk acts as an emulsifier for the oil and water-based ingredients, while the cornstarch provides the extra clinging power.

4. **Temper and combine** Slowly pour the warm sauce mixture into the egg yolk slurry while whisking continuously to temper the egg and prevent scrambling. Mix until fully combined.

5. **Simmer and thicken** Pour the entire combined mixture back into the saucepan. Bring to a gentle simmer over medium-low heat, whisking constantly, and let it simmer for 1 to 2 minutes to fully activate the extra cornstarch and lock in the thickness. Remove from heat.

6. **Cool and store** Allow the sauce to cool completely before transferring to your 16oz squeeze bottle. Refrigerate until ready to use. Reheat gently before tossing with cooked wings.

---

### Shelf Life & Storage

- **Refrigerated:** 2–3 weeks in an airtight container  
- **Freezing:** Not recommended (emulsion may break)  
- **Food Safety Notes:**  
  - Discard if the sauce develops off odors, visible mold, or excessive separation that does not re-emulsify when warmed and stirred.
  - Always use clean utensils when scooping to maximize shelf life.

## Butter Based Sauce

This version is best when you want a **classic, traditional buffalo wing experience** with rich butter flavor and a clingy coating designed to be used immediately.

Use this recipe when:
- You are tossing wings **fresh out of the fryer or oven**
- The sauce will be **served warm and consumed the same day**
- You want a **rich, buttery mouthfeel** with bold heat and tang
- Minimal ingredients and simplicity are preferred
- Ultra-processed ingredients are being avoided as much as possible

Characteristics:
- Thinner when hot, firms up when refrigerated
- Butter provides natural richness and cling but is not shelf-stable
- Best texture and flavor when freshly made
- May separate slightly when cooled and reheated

This is the ideal choice for **classic wing nights**, small batches, and situations where authenticity and freshness outweigh long-term storage.

### Yield

- Approximately **3/4 cup** of sauce  
- Enough for **1.5–2 lb of wings**, depending on coating preference

---

### Prep Time

- **Prep:** 3 minutes  
- **Cook:** 7 minutes  
- **Total:** ~10 minutes

---

### Ingredients

- 1/2 cup Frank’s cayenne pepper sauce (e.g., Frank’s RedHot Original)
- 1/4 cup salted butter
- 1 tablespoon white vinegar
- 1/2 teaspoon Worcestershire sauce
- 1/4 teaspoon garlic powder
- 1/4 teaspoon paprika
- 1/8 teaspoon onion powder
- 1/8 teaspoon cayenne pepper (adjust to preferred spiciness)

---

### Instructions

1. **Melt the butter**  
   In a small saucepan, melt the salted butter over low heat.

2. **Add remaining ingredients**  
   Add the cayenne pepper sauce, white vinegar, Worcestershire sauce, garlic powder, paprika, onion powder, and cayenne pepper. Stir to combine.

3. **Simmer the sauce**  
   Let the sauce simmer gently over low heat for 5–7 minutes, whisking occasionally. Do not let it boil.

4. **Taste and adjust**  
   Taste the sauce. If desired, add a pinch of salt, though salted butter typically provides sufficient seasoning.

5. **Serve warm**  
   Toss with freshly cooked wings immediately or use as a dipping sauce while warm.

---

### Shelf Life & Storage

- **Refrigerated:** Up to 1 week in an airtight container  
- **Freezing:** Not recommended (butter may separate)  
- **Food Safety Notes:**  
  - Sauce will firm up when cold; reheat gently to restore texture.  
  - Always use clean utensils when scooping to maximize shelf life.  
  - Discard if off odors or mold appear.
